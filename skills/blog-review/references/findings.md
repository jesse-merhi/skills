# Writing Finding Bar

A finding must identify:

1. a specific location;
2. observable evidence in the article;
3. a reader-facing consequence;
4. a revision direction that addresses the cause;
5. confidence that the issue is not merely personal taste.

## Severity

- `P1`: the article's meaning, factual trust, central argument, or narrative arc
  is materially broken.
- `P2`: a section-level problem substantially weakens comprehension,
  engagement, voice, or pacing.
- `P3`: a local but concrete flow, redundancy, ambiguity, or voice problem that
  a reader will notice.

## Exclude

- an alternative sentence with no demonstrated improvement;
- generic advice such as “make this punchier” or “add more personality”;
- personal dislike of ellipses, capitalization, fragments, or informality;
- a readability score without a located reading problem;
- AI-detection output without textual evidence;
- factual suspicion that can be checked but has not been checked;
- the same root cause repeated for every sentence it affects.

## Deduplication

Fingerprint a finding by section, writing function, and root cause. If three
sentences repeat a redundant reversal pattern, record one pattern finding with
the strongest examples rather than three line edits. Keep a separate finding
only when the reader impact or required repair differs.
