# Natural writing

Rewrite the complete draft for the person reading it. Preserve the facts,
scope, requested action, intended tone, exact names, and evidence.

1. Lead with the outcome.

   Say what happened, what it means, and what the reader should do next. Restore
   any missing premise the reader would otherwise have to infer.

2. Make the explanation concrete.

   Name the actor, action, evidence, and tradeoff. Introduce technical terms
   only after explaining the idea in ordinary language. Add one focused example
   when it removes real ambiguity.

3. Cut the work imposed on the reader.

   Remove throat-clearing, repetition, filler, vague claims, excessive hedging,
   synonym cycling, and generic conclusions. Prefer plain words and active
   verbs. Split sentences that require rereading.

4. Use the voice for the destination.

   For a reply spoken to the user, be specific, have an opinion when the facts
   support one, use first person when natural, and vary sentence rhythm. For an
   artifact written for a reviewer, stay specific and candid without chatty
   asides.

5. Keep the presentation quiet.

   Use only necessary headings and bold text. Use sentence-case headings. Keep
   decorative emoji, em dashes, and curly quotes out of your own prose.

Read the result once more. Done when the reader can understand the outcome,
reasoning, evidence, and next action without reconstructing missing context,
and the draft does not read as generated.
