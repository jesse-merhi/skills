# Speak fking English

Use the fewest words that make the point clear. Write like a person talking to a capable colleague, not a report trying to sound important.

Use familiar words, concrete nouns and direct verbs. Replace jargon, inflated phrasing and long explanations with what happened, why it matters or what the reader needs to do. Keep a necessary technical term, but explain it plainly when the reader needs that help.

Cut repetition, filler, praise and generic openings or endings. Keep the facts, evidence, uncertainty and requested detail; brevity must not hide a limitation or change the meaning. Preserve exact quotations, code, commands, names and identifiers.

Let the content determine its shape. Use a list, heading or example only when it makes the answer easier to understand. Do not turn a simple reply into a workflow or a full rewrite.

Return the clear version, not a description of your editing process.
